Before any model was trained, the team wrote down every model and every evaluation the study would run. This lesson reads that plan: how it is generated, how models are named, what the scales and the training budget are, and what it all costs on one GPU.

5.1One script fixes the whole experiment

run_matrix.py multiplies five short lists together and writes the result to two CSV files: models.csv, one row per model, and run_matrix.csv, one row per (model, diagnostic) evaluation. Once these files were committed, rows could only ever be marked done or skipped, never added. That bounds the study before any compute is spent, and stops anyone from quietly adding a model after seeing results.

Every file the project writes about a model is named after its model_id, built from the five axes in a fixed order. A diagnostic run adds the diagnostic to it.

5.3What it costs: measured throughput

The budget had to fit one A10G GPU per team member, so the team measured it. throughput.sh trains every architecture and scale for 300 timed steps, and train.py extrapolates the seconds per step to the full 5,000. The results are in src/docs/throughput.md, and this chart is drawn from that file.

One domain is 81 models, about 37 GPU hours of training; all three domains come to about 111. Mamba is 88% of that, and the 9 Mamba large models of one domain take about 22 hours on their own. The team kept the full matrix, including Mamba large, and split the Othello zoo across four machines (Lesson 7).

Why not train longer?

At this budget the large transformer reaches a legal move rate of 0.969 on validation games, where Othello-GPT, trained far longer, reaches 0.999. The preregistration keeps the budget anyway: four times the budget would cost about 150 GPU hours for Othello alone, and the study needs models of different quality, because rank correlations between diagnostics only mean something when the models differ.

5.4The held-out domain

H4 asks whether the findings carry over to something the analysis never saw. The team decided on 5 October to hold out one whole domain, the interpreter: its models are trained and scored only after H1 to H3 are settled on Othello and navigation. No architecture is held out, because with only three families, holding one out would remove a third of the models from every H1–H3 analysis and leave only two families to explain disagreement with in H2.
