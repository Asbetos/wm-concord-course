D1 is the test most papers start and stop with: does the model only propose legal moves? It is the simplest of the four and the weakest, and the study includes it to measure exactly how much weaker. Everything it needs is already in Lesson 3.

10.1The idea

Take any prefix of a test game, say D3 C3 C4. The rules engine replays it and lists the legal next moves: C5 and E3. The model reads the same prefix and proposes its most likely next move. If that move is C5 or E3, the position counts as valid, even if the recorded game went on with the other one. If the model proposes anything else, say C3, which is already occupied, the position is invalid. D1 repeats this at every position of 1,000 test games.

The model does not need to guess the move the players chose; it only has to stay inside the rules. That is why D1 sits far above next-token accuracy (Lesson 6): about 0.94 against 0.15 for a medium transformer.

10.2The score

  • 96Find the model’s own training distribution: from models.csv for a zoo model, from its metadata for an anchor.
  • 97Load 1,000 test games of that distribution and build them with legal moves (Lesson 3).
  • 98The score: othello.legal_rate(), the share of positions where the top move is legal. The same function decided admission on validation games; here it runs on test games.
  • 99A second, sampled rate, kept in details only (section 10.3).
  • 100The number of positions scored: every position that has a recorded next move, about 58 per game.

10.3The second number: sampling instead of argmax

The top move hides how much probability the model puts on illegal moves. A model can rank a legal move first while still giving illegal moves 30% of its probability. So D1 also draws 10 moves at every position from the model’s full distribution (temperature 1.0, a fixed random seed) and reports the share of draws that are legal.

  • 68One seeded random generator for the whole run, so the same model always gets the same draws.
  • 71–75Turn the scores into probabilities and draw 10 moves per position, with replacement.
  • 78–82For the same positions legal_rate() scores, look up whether each drawn token is legal and count.

10.5What D1 cannot see

  • Boards it confuses.A model can propose a legal move at almost every position while mixing up boards that allow the same few moves. D1 looks at one position at a time and cannot notice. D2 compares pairs of positions to catch exactly this (Lesson 11).
  • Competence in disguise.D1 on test games is nearly the same number as the validation legal move rate that admitted the model, which the study calls competence. That is why the H1 pairs that include D1 can be marked not interpretable once competence is controlled, and why H3 treats D1 as the reference that the other diagnostics must beat (Lesson 8).
